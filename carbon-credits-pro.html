💡 Understanding Your Credits KPI Dashboard

Carbon Deficit
This is the gap between your current emissions trajectory and your net-zero target. It represents the volume of emissions you still need to eliminate through either operational reductions or carbon sequestration/offset measures.
Example: If you emit 216,417 tCO₂e now and target 108,209 tCO₂e by 2030, but have only achieved 67,098 tCO₂e in reductions, your deficit is 41,119 tCO₂e.
Sequestration Target
The total amount of carbon you plan to capture through nature-based solutions by your target year. This is typically achieved through Track-B projects like forest restoration, agroforestry, or mangrove conservation.
Example: You've set a target of 45,000 tCO₂e sequestration by 2030, which exceeds your 41,119 tCO₂e deficit, providing a safety buffer.
Active Projects
The count of sequestration initiatives currently deployed and monitored. Each project contributes verified carbon capture capacity toward your overall sequestration target.
Example: 3 projects = Forest Restoration (25,000 tCO₂e) + Agroforestry (12,000 tCO₂e) + Mangrove Conservation (8,000 tCO₂e).
Projected Surplus
The excess carbon sequestration capacity beyond your deficit. This buffer accounts for project variability, verification uncertainties, and ensures you meet commitments even if some projects underperform.
Example: With 45,000 tCO₂e sequestration against 41,119 tCO₂e deficit, you have a 3,881 tCO₂e surplus (~9.4% buffer).
Current Emissions
Your organization's total greenhouse gas emissions for the current reporting period, measured in tonnes of CO₂ equivalent (tCO₂e). This serves as the baseline for tracking reduction progress.
Example: 216,417 tCO₂e includes all Scope 1, 2, and applicable Scope 3 emissions across your operations.
Achieved Reductions
The cumulative carbon savings realized through operational improvements, efficiency measures, renewable energy adoption, and process optimization. These are permanent reductions in your emissions footprint.
Example: 67,098 tCO₂e achieved through energy efficiency projects, fleet electrification, and renewable energy procurement.
Target Emissions
Your science-based or commitment-driven emissions goal for the target year. This represents the maximum allowable emissions level to be considered on track toward net-zero.
Example: 108,209 tCO₂e by 2030 represents a 50% reduction from your baseline, aligned with 1.5°C pathways.
Deficit Percentage
The proportion of your current emissions that still needs to be addressed to meet your target. A lower percentage indicates better progress toward your decarbonization goals.
Example: 19% deficit means you still need to address approximately one-fifth of your current emissions through further reductions or sequestration.
Total Sequestration
The combined verified carbon capture capacity from all your active sequestration projects. This value offsets your remaining emissions deficit and moves you toward carbon neutrality.
Example: 45,000 tCO₂e total from all three projects, verified through Track-B monitoring protocols.
Net Position
The final carbon balance after accounting for reductions, sequestration, and any credit purchases. A positive value means you're carbon positive; negative means additional action is needed.
Example: +3,881 tCO₂e indicates you have excess sequestration capacity, providing confidence in meeting your net-zero commitment.
