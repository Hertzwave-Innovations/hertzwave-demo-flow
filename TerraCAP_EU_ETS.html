Monitoring Plan

The Monitoring Plan (MP) is the cornerstone of EU ETS compliance. It defines exactly how your installation measures and reports emissions — which source streams, which tiers, which calculation factors. It must be approved by your Competent Authority before the first reporting period and updated when material changes occur.
⚠
MP approval is a prerequisite. You cannot submit a valid Annual Emissions Report until your Competent Authority has approved your Monitoring Plan. Any changes to the MP (new source streams, tier changes, methodology updates) require CA re-approval before the next reporting period.

Change trigger (if amendment)

Section B — Monitoring methodology
Primary methodology
Calculation-based is standard for most industries. CEMS (Continuous Emission Monitoring Systems) requires additional equipment but can reduce uncertainty.
Tier applied (activity data)
Higher tiers require more rigorous measurement but reduce uncertainty. Category A & B installations may apply for lower tiers under MRR Art. 17.
Source streams (fuel types and process inputs)
List all significant source streams as defined in the approved MP. Each stream needs its own activity data, NCV (if fuel), and emission factor.

ℹ
Key difference from India PAT/CCTS: The Monitoring Plan has no direct equivalent in the Indian system. PAT/CCTS uses BEE DC registration and sector-specific M&V guidelines. EU ETS requires a facility-specific, CA-approved document governing every measurement decision — this is the legal foundation for the AER.

Annual Emissions Report

The Annual Emissions Report (AER) quantifies your installation's verified GHG emissions for the reporting year, calculated per the approved Monitoring Plan. Submitted to your Competent Authority by 31 March each year. Must be verified before submission — same deadline.

Verification

EU ETS verification is mandatory every year. The verifier is an independent body accredited under EN ISO 14065 and EU Accreditation and Verification Regulation AVR (Reg 2018/2067 as amended by Reg 2023/2124). Verification must be complete before you submit the AER to your Competent Authority — both are due 31 March.
⚠
Not the same as CCTS ACVA. EU ETS verifiers are accredited under the EU Accreditation and Verification Regulation (AVR 2018/2067) and must be notified to the European Commission. BEE-empanelled ACVAs used for India CCTS are not recognised for EU ETS. A separate EU-accredited verifier must be appointed.

Verification opinion
"Reasonable assurance" is the standard outcome for a compliant installation. Qualified opinions trigger CA review and possible penalty proceedings.
What the EU ETS verifier reviews
Verification scope
✓ Monitoring Plan implementation and CA approval status
✓ Activity data measurement against approved tiers
✓ Source stream completeness — no omissions
✓ Emission factor appropriateness (MRR Annex II or lab analysis)
✓ Biomass CO₂ zero-rating eligibility per RED II
✓ Oxidation factor verification per source stream
✓ Calculation methodology consistency with MRR
Materiality threshold
Category A: 5% or 2,000 tCO₂e (whichever lower)
Category B: 2% or 5,000 tCO₂e (whichever lower)
Category C: 2% of verified emissions
Errors below materiality: corrected in AER. Above: qualified opinion.
Site visit requirement
Mandatory in year 1 and at least every 2 years thereafter. Remote assessment permitted for low-risk installations.
Qualified opinion — corrective action required
A qualified opinion means the verifier identified a material misstatement. You must: (1) correct the AER and re-submit to CA, (2) document the misstatement and correction, (3) obtain a revised verification statement before surrender deadline.

Free allocation

EU ETS is a cap-and-trade system. Every installation receives a number of free EU Allowances (EUAs) based on a product benchmark and historical production. Free allocation is being reduced for CBAM-covered sectors to prevent double protection — enter your data below to calculate your position.
⚠
CBAM impact on free allocation. For cement, steel, aluminium, fertilisers, hydrogen, and electricity, free allocation is being phased out 2026–2034 as CBAM provides equivalent carbon leakage protection. By 2034, CBAM sectors receive zero free allocation. The calculator below shows the year-by-year impact.

⚠

Installations with multiple product benchmarks (e.g. cement clinker + heat export) must calculate allocation per sub-installation separately. This module currently supports a single sub-installation. Multi-sub-installation modelling is on the roadmap.

Surrender & compliance

By 30 April each year, you must surrender a number of EU Allowances (EUAs) equal to your verified emissions for the previous year. If your free allocation covers your emissions, your net cost is zero or negative (surplus EUAs can be sold). If not, you must purchase EUAs on the carbon market.

ℹ
Key difference from India PAT/CCTS: EU ETS requires surrender of one EUA per tonne of verified emissions (absolute cap). EU ETS compliance cost is therefore directly linked to production volume in a way CCTS compliance is not.

CBAM interaction — EU ETS free allocation phase-out
For CBAM-covered goods (cement, steel, aluminium, fertilisers, hydrogen, electricity), your free allocation is being reduced annually from 2026 to zero by 2034. The CBAM charges your EU importers an equivalent carbon price on their imports, replacing the protection previously provided by free allocation.

Practical impact: If you export CBAM-covered goods to EU customers, your free allocation reduces — but your EU customers bear the CBAM certificate cost. This cost may affect bilateral pricing negotiations — the actual commercial effect depends on contract terms, market conditions, and the relative bargaining position of buyer and supplier. TerraCAP's CBAM supplier package (Step 8 in the CMC module) provides the verified embedded-emissions data your EU customers need to calculate their CBAM liability.
