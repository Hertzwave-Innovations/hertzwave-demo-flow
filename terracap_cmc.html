India's carbon compliance landscape
Three regulatory regimes govern carbon and energy reporting for Indian industry. PAT came first in 2012, CCTS is replacing it sector by sector from 2025, and CBAM is the EU border mechanism that consumes the output of both.

PAT
Perform Achieve & Trade
Bureau of Energy Efficiency (BEE) · 2012 onward · Cycle VII active
India's foundational energy efficiency compliance regime. Designated Consumers (DCs) — large energy-intensive facilities notified by BEE — receive a Specific Energy Consumption (SEC) target in Tonne of Oil Equivalent (TOE) per unit of output. Over-performers earn Energy Saving Certificates (ESCerts) tradeable on IEX and PXIL. PAT is being phased out sector-by-sector as CCTS is notified.
Designated Consumer (DC) thresholds — sector-specific
Cement: >30,000 Tonne of Oil Equivalent (TOE)/year
Iron & Steel: >50,000 TOE/year
Aluminium, fertiliser, textile: sector-specific cutoffs (not a single universal rule)
Currently Cycle VII: FY2022–23 to FY2025–26
Metric: SEC (TOE/unit output)Certificate: ESCert

CCTS
Carbon Credit Trading Scheme
MoEFCC / BEE · Notified Oct 2025 + Jan 2026 amendments
India's GHG-intensity compliance scheme replacing PAT for notified sectors. Facilities below their sector intensity target earn Carbon Credit Certificates (CCCs) they can sell on exchanges. Facilities above must purchase CCCs or face penalties under the Carbon Credit Trading Rules 2023.
Notified sectors — by notification status
Oct 2025: Cement, Aluminium (primary), Chlor-alkali, Pulp & Paper — baseline year FY2023–24
Jan 2026 amendment: Secondary aluminium, Petroleum refinery, Petrochemicals, Textiles — baseline year FY2024–25
Anticipated: Iron & Steel, Fertilisers — BEE roadmap, timeline TBD
Metric: GHG intensity (tCO₂e/unit)Certificate: CCC

CBAM
Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism
EU Regulation 2023/956 · Definitive phase from 1 January 2026
EU's border carbon price on carbon-intensive imports. From January 2026, the legal declarant obligation sits with the EU-side authorized CBAM declarant. Indian exporters supply verified embedded-emissions data to support those declarations. TerraCAP generates this Supplier Emissions Package — pulled directly from your CCTS/PAT calculation.

Verification & ACVA handoff
All CCTS and PAT submissions require independent third-party verification by a BEE-empanelled Accredited Carbon Verification Agency (ACVA) before certificates are issued. TerraCAP manages this in four distinct phases with clear owner assignments.
What is an ACVA? — Accredited Carbon Verification Agency
An ACVA is a BEE-empanelled third-party auditor — similar to a statutory auditor but for carbon and energy data. They examine your boundary definition, data integrity, emission factor selection, and intensity calculation before BEE will accept your submission and issue certificates. Bureau Veritas, SGS, DNV, and TÜV are examples of BEE-empanelled ACVAs.
Phase 1 of 4
Document preparation
Owner: TerraCAP + Plant team
Filing document, source data export, audit trail, boundary statement, and emission factor references packaged automatically by TerraCAP. Plant DGM Environment reviews and approves. Hours vs weeks manually.
Phase 2 of 4
Verifier interaction
Owner: ACVA + Plant team
ACVA selected from BEE empanelled list. Site review conducted — typically 4–6 weeks. ACVA raises clarifications in TerraCAP's comment thread; plant responds without rebuilding the filing.
Phase 3 of 4
Verification statement
Owner: ACVA
ACVA issues formal verification statement per ISO 14064-3 / ISAE 3410. TerraCAP attaches it to the submission bundle. Statement references compliance period, boundary, intensity, and production data.
Phase 4 of 4
Regulator submission
Owner: Plant team + BEE
Verified bundle submitted to BEE portal. BEE accepts, issues certificates to the facility's account. TerraCAP portfolio updates in real time post-issuance.
