💡 Understanding Credit Inventory Management

What is Credit Inventory?
Credit inventory is your real-time registry of all carbon credits you own or control. It tracks each credit's status, origin, vintage, expiration, and transaction history, providing complete visibility and traceability for compliance and auditing.
Serial Number Tracking
Each credit batch receives a unique serial number from the issuing registry (e.g., Verra, Gold Standard). This ensures credits are uniquely identifiable, prevents double-counting, and maintains an audit trail from issuance through retirement.
Available vs. Reserved Status
Available credits are ready for any use. Reserved credits are earmarked for specific purposes (e.g., annual compliance report) but not yet retired. Reserving ensures you have sufficient credits allocated while maintaining flexibility before formal retirement.
Credit Expiration
While credits don't technically "expire," many compliance programs only accept credits within specific vintage windows (e.g., credits from last 5 years). Tracking expiration helps ensure timely use before credits lose compliance value.
Transaction History
Every inventory movement—purchases, retirements, transfers, reservations—is logged with timestamps, quantities, and notes. This audit trail is essential for compliance verification, financial reporting, and demonstrating chain of custody.
Registry Synchronization
HWCAP syncs with official credit registries to verify holdings and reflect official retirements. Regular sync ensures your inventory matches registry records, preventing discrepancies and maintaining data integrity for audits.
Inventory Optimization
Regular inventory review helps optimize holdings by identifying expiring credits, balancing vintage diversity, ensuring sufficient reserves for upcoming compliance periods, and avoiding over-purchasing that ties up capital unnecessarily.
Batch Management
Credits are managed in batches based on project, vintage, and purchase transaction. Batch-level tracking enables precise cost accounting, FIFO/LIFO retirement strategies, and granular reporting on which specific credits were used for what purpose.
