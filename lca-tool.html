📘 What is Life Cycle Assessment (LCA)?

LCA is a standardized methodology (ISO 14040 / ISO 14044) that quantifies the environmental impacts of a product, process, or service across its entire life — from raw material extraction through manufacturing, distribution, use, and end-of-life disposal. Unlike your organizational GHG inventory (Scope 1/2/3), LCA operates at the product level, answering: "What is the environmental footprint of a specific product we make?"

  • GWP (Global Warming Potential) — Measures total greenhouse gas impact in kgCO₂-equivalent. This is the primary climate metric in LCA, equivalent to your carbon footprint per product.
  • Acidification, Eutrophication, Water Depletion — Additional environmental impacts beyond carbon that regulators (CSRD, BRSR) increasingly require.
  • Cradle-to-Gate vs Cradle-to-Grave — "Gate" means your factory exit; "Grave" means end-of-life disposal. CBAM requires Cradle-to-Gate; CSRD recommends Cradle-to-Grave.

Key Findings:

1. API synthesis dominates environmental impact — 42% of GWP (Global Warming Potential), 55% of eutrophication, 30% of acidification. The 4-Aminophenol route is energy and solvent-intensive. Switching to catalytic hydrogenation could reduce API-stage GWP by ~18-25%.

2. Grid electricity is the second-largest GWP contributor (28%). Telangana DISCOM grid EF: 0.82 kgCO₂e/kWh. Increasing rooftop solar from 21% to 60% would cut manufacturing-stage GWP by ~35% (overall product GWP reduction: ~12%).

3. Water footprint driven by cooling (64%). Closed-loop cooling would reduce water consumption ~40% with 18-month payback.

4. CBAM exposure: At 7.82 kgCO₂e/1000 tabs, embedded carbon is within pharma sector range. Applying combined scenarios could save ~₹2.4L/year in CBAM certificate costs at projected EU ETS prices.
