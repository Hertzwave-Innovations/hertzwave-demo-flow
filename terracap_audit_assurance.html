1Engagement Details
✓ Limited Assurance (selected)
The auditor performs mainly analytical procedures and inquiry — not full testing of every item. Their opinion says nothing has come to their attention to suggest the data is materially misstated ("negative assurance"). Lower cost and time; the standard starting point for most first-time BRSR/GHG assurance engagements.
Reasonable Assurance
The auditor performs testing and evidence-gathering comparable to a financial statement audit. Their opinion is a positive, direct statement that the data is fairly stated ("positive assurance"). Higher cost and time, and higher assurance value to stakeholders — typically required for BRSR Core "reasonable assurance" parameters mandated for top-listed entities.
Suggested from the entity's jurisdiction (India) and reporting period against TerraCAP's Standards Registry. ICAI has not yet finalised its SSA-5000 adoption date, so engagements straddling 15 Dec 2026 need Engagement Partner confirmation. View Standards Registry →
What actually changes with the standard you pick:
  • The audit procedures the auditor is required to follow — risk assessment, materiality-setting, and sampling rules differ between the ISAE 3000 family and the ISSA/SSA 5000 family (the latter adds explicit double-materiality assessment).
  • The wording and format of the assurance opinion itself, which regulators and stakeholders read literally — an opinion issued under the wrong standard can be rejected on a technicality even if the underlying work was sound.
  • Whether SEBI/regulatory filings accept it — BRSR Core assurance must be performed to a standard SEBI recognises for the filing year in question.
  • Little to no change to what data the auditor asks you for — evidence and access requirements stay broadly similar across standards.

4Management Disclosure & Completeness Declaration
📝 Why this exists
This is management's formal, signed representation to the audit team — similar in weight to your Independence Declaration on the auditor's side. It discloses known data-quality issues, control weaknesses, estimation methods, exclusions, and methodology changes. It does not set any risk rating — the auditor rates risk independently in their Assurance Risk Assessment. This declaration is version-locked once signed; any later change is tracked, not overwritten.
Enter the person authorised to make representations to the auditor on behalf of the entity. This does not have to be a pre-listed role — any name can be entered, subject to the authorisation basis below.
The auditor's engagement partner may request supporting documentation for the authorisation basis before accepting this declaration as management's representation.
Once signed: this declaration is versioned and date-stamped. Auditor access to TerraAudit will not be activated until this step is complete and the audit firm's Independence Declaration is received (Step 5). Any edit after fieldwork begins creates a new version, an alert on affected risk rows, and a timeline entry — it does not silently overwrite the signed record.

🛡️ Limited Assurance Statement — Draft Preview
INDEPENDENT LIMITED ASSURANCE REPORT
on the Greenhouse Gas Emissions Statement
Acme Manufacturing Ltd and Subsidiaries · FY 2024-25
DRAFT — Subject to Partner Review

To the Board of Directors of Acme Manufacturing Ltd

Scope

We have undertaken a limited assurance engagement on the GHG Emissions Statement ("the Statement") of Acme Manufacturing Ltd and its subsidiaries (collectively, "the Group") for the year ended March 31, 2025. The Statement comprises Scope 1 direct emissions (380.3 tCO₂e), Scope 2 location-based indirect emissions (12.3 tCO₂e), and selected Scope 3 value chain emissions (8,704.6 tCO₂e) as prepared in accordance with the GHG Protocol Corporate Standard (Revised).

Criteria

The criteria against which the Statement has been assessed are: the GHG Protocol Corporate Accounting and Reporting Standard (Revised Edition), the India GHG Program Guidelines, IPCC Guidelines for National GHG Inventories (2006, as updated in 2019), and the applicable reporting requirements of SEBI BRSR Core, ISSB S2, and CSRD/ESRS E1.

Management's Responsibility

Management is responsible for the preparation of the Statement in accordance with the above criteria, including the design, implementation, and maintenance of internal controls relevant to the preparation of a GHG Statement that is free from material misstatement.

Our Responsibility

Our responsibility is to express a limited assurance conclusion on the Statement based on the procedures we have performed and evidence obtained. We conducted our limited assurance engagement in accordance with ISAE 3410 — Assurance Engagements on Greenhouse Gas Statements, issued by the IAASB. This standard requires that we plan and perform our procedures to obtain limited assurance about whether the Statement is free from material misstatement.

Summary of Work Performed

Our procedures included: inquiries of management and personnel; analytical procedures on reported emissions data and trends; inspection of supporting evidence including utility invoices, fuel purchase records, and travel data; evaluation of the appropriateness of quantification methodologies and emission factors; reconciliation of reported data to underlying records via TerraCAP data lineage; and assessment of the Group's data quality controls.

Conclusion

Based on the procedures we have performed and the evidence we have obtained, nothing has come to our attention that causes us to believe that the GHG Emissions Statement of the Group for the year ended March 31, 2025 is not prepared, in all material respects, in accordance with the applicable criteria.

Emphasis of Matter

Without qualifying our conclusion, we draw attention to: (a) a diesel consumption anomaly at Site 3 in November 2024 (Finding F-001) for which management has provided an explanation, and (b) the use of AR4 GWP values for R-410A refrigerant (Finding F-003) which management has committed to updating in the next reporting cycle.

📋 Management Letter — Draft Preview
MANAGEMENT LETTER
Engagement AUD-2025-001 · FY 2024-25
DRAFT

Dear Management,

In connection with our limited assurance engagement on the GHG Emissions Statement, we wish to bring the following matters to your attention. These findings and recommendations are intended to assist management in strengthening its carbon accounting and reporting processes.

Finding F-001 — Critical: Diesel Consumption Anomaly

Generator diesel consumption at Site 3 in November 2024 (8,500 litres) exceeded the DG set rated capacity (2,500 litres/month) by 340%. This resulted in an unexplained spike in Scope 1 emissions.

Recommendation: Implement automated consumption threshold alerts in TerraCAP, require supervisor approval for entries exceeding ±30% of the rolling 3-month average, and conduct a root cause analysis for the November anomaly.

Finding F-002 — Major: Incomplete Scope 3 Category 6

Rail travel emissions are excluded from the Scope 3 business travel category, leading to an estimated understatement of 12–18 tCO₂e.

Recommendation: Integrate SAP travel module data with TerraCAP to automatically capture all travel modes. Establish quarterly completeness checks for all Scope 3 categories.

Finding F-003 — Major: Outdated GWP Values for Refrigerants

R-410A GWP is calculated using AR4 value (2,088) rather than the current AR6 value (2,256), resulting in an understatement of approximately 7.5 tCO₂e.

Recommendation: Update TerraCAP emission factor library to AR6 GWP values. Implement an annual review process for emission factor currency.

Finding F-004 — Minor: BRSR Intensity Denominator

Emission intensity is reported using revenue in some sections and production output in others. Standardize to a single primary denominator with supplementary metrics.

Finding F-005 — Observation: Scope 1 Trend Narrative

Scope 1 emissions show an 8% year-on-year increase without an accompanying narrative explanation. We recommend including variance analysis narratives for all significant year-on-year changes (>5%).

Improvement Opportunities

Beyond the formal findings above, we note the following opportunities for the Group to strengthen its sustainability reporting maturity:

• Implement real-time IoT-based monitoring for fuel consumption at all DG sets to replace manual log entry.

• Establish a formal uncertainty assessment methodology for Scope 3 purchased goods estimates.

• Consider transitioning to market-based Scope 2 reporting alongside the current location-based approach.

We would be pleased to discuss any of the above matters at your convenience.
