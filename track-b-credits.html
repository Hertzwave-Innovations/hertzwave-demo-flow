💡 Understanding Sequestration Projects & Track-B Credits

What are Sequestration Projects?
Sequestration projects are nature-based initiatives you own or manage that actively remove CO₂ from the atmosphere. Common types include forest restoration, agroforestry, mangrove conservation, and soil carbon enhancement. These projects generate Track-B credits.
Track-A vs. Track-B Credits
Track-A credits are purchased from third-party projects in the open market. Track-B credits are self-generated from your own sequestration projects. Track-B credits demonstrate direct investment in climate solutions and stronger ESG commitment.
Project Monitoring Requirements
Regular monitoring (typically quarterly) tracks carbon sequestration rates, project health, and compliance with methodologies. Monitoring includes field measurements, satellite imagery, biodiversity assessments, and community engagement verification.
Third-Party Verification
Independent verification (annually or bi-annually) validates monitoring data and confirms carbon sequestration claims. Verified credits can be formally issued and used for compliance or voluntary offsetting with full credibility.
Carbon Sequestration Rates
Sequestration rates vary by ecosystem: forests capture 2-10 tCO₂e/ha/year, agroforestry 1-5 tCO₂e/ha/year, mangroves 5-15 tCO₂e/ha/year, and grasslands 0.5-3 tCO₂e/ha/year. Rates depend on species, climate, soil quality, and management practices.
Project Timeline & Permanence
Sequestration projects require long-term commitment (typically 20-40 years minimum) to ensure carbon storage permanence. Projects must include plans for fire prevention, disease management, and legal protections to prevent reversal of carbon gains.
Co-Benefits Beyond Carbon
Sequestration projects often provide additional benefits: biodiversity enhancement, watershed protection, soil health improvement, community livelihoods, and climate adaptation. These co-benefits strengthen ESG profiles and stakeholder value.
Additionality & Baseline
To generate Track-B credits, projects must demonstrate additionality—carbon sequestration wouldn't occur without the project. A baseline scenario documents expected carbon levels without intervention, proving the project's incremental climate benefit.
Leakage Prevention
Leakage occurs when carbon benefits in one area cause increased emissions elsewhere (e.g., protecting one forest shifts deforestation to another). Projects must monitor and account for leakage to ensure net positive climate impact.
Credit Issuance Process
After verification, credits are issued to your account in approved registries (e.g., Verra, Gold Standard). Issued Track-B credits can be held for future use, transferred to other entities, or retired for compliance/voluntary claims—just like Track-A credits.
Project Risk Management
Risks include natural disasters (fire, drought, pests), social challenges (land tenure disputes, community opposition), and methodology changes. Risk mitigation includes insurance, buffer pools, diversification across project types, and robust community engagement.
Compliance Recognition
Track-B credits from properly verified sequestration projects are increasingly recognized in compliance frameworks like CBAM, voluntary standards like SBTi, and corporate net-zero commitments. Ensure your methodology aligns with relevant standards.
