Carbon Offset Strategy

Carbon offsets represent investments in projects that remove or avoid greenhouse gas emissions elsewhere, helping bridge the gap between your current emissions and NetZero targets. While direct emissions reductions should always be prioritized, high-quality offsets play a strategic role in achieving your climate goals, particularly for residual emissions that are difficult or impossible to eliminate.
🔗 Connecting Offsets to Your Climate Goals
Your offset strategy works in tandem with your NetZero commitment and interim milestones. As you progress toward your target year, offsets can help you:
  • Bridge reduction gaps: Address emissions that remain after implementing reduction measures
  • Achieve interim targets: Meet 2030 or 2035 milestones while longer-term reduction investments mature
  • Neutralize residual emissions: Handle unavoidable emissions in your final NetZero year (e.g., essential business travel, certain industrial processes)
📋 Types of Carbon Offset Projects:
🌳 Nature-Based Solutions
Reforestation, afforestation, mangrove restoration, and soil carbon sequestration projects that remove CO₂ from the atmosphere while supporting biodiversity
Example: Restoring 500 hectares of degraded forest in the Western Ghats, sequestering ~3,500 tCO₂e annually
⚡ Renewable Energy
Wind farms, solar installations, and hydroelectric projects that displace fossil fuel-based electricity generation
Example: 50 MW solar project in Rajasthan avoiding ~80,000 tCO₂e annually by replacing coal power
🏭 Industrial Efficiency
Energy efficiency improvements, waste heat recovery, and fuel switching projects in manufacturing facilities
Example: Methane capture from landfills preventing ~25,000 tCO₂e annually from entering the atmosphere
🔬 Technology-Based Removal
Direct air capture (DAC), biochar production, and enhanced weathering that permanently remove CO₂
Example: Direct air capture facility with geological storage removing 1,000 tCO₂e per year with 1,000+ year permanence
⚖️ Quality Standards for Carbon Offsets:
High-quality offsets should be verified by recognized standards such as: Verra (VCS), Gold Standard, Climate Action Reserve (CAR), or American Carbon Registry (ACR). Ensure projects demonstrate additionality (wouldn't happen without carbon finance), permanence (long-term carbon storage), and no double-counting (credits claimed by only one entity). For alignment with Science-Based Targets, prioritize carbon removal projects over avoidance projects, especially as you approach your NetZero year.

Revenue & Financial Metrics

📊 How This Data is Used
Revenue and production data help calculate "carbon intensity" - a key metric that shows how efficiently you operate:
  • Emissions per Revenue: Shows carbon footprint relative to business size (e.g., 10 tCO₂e per ₹1 Crore revenue). Used in BRSR, CDP, and investor reports.
  • Emissions per Production Unit: Shows efficiency of manufacturing (e.g., 0.5 tCO₂e per unit produced). Critical for tracking operational improvements.
  • Emissions per Employee: Useful for service sectors to benchmark against peers.
Why this matters: As your business grows, absolute emissions might increase, but if intensity decreases, you're becoming more carbon-efficient. Investors and regulators look at both metrics.

Carbon Pricing & Budget

💡 Why Carbon Pricing Matters
Internal Carbon Price: This is what your organization assigns as the "cost" of emitting one tonne of CO₂. It's used to:
  • Evaluate investments: When deciding between projects, carbon price helps quantify climate impact in financial terms
  • Drive behavior: Departments with budgets factor in carbon costs, incentivizing lower-emission choices
  • Future-proof decisions: Many governments are implementing carbon taxes. Your internal price helps prepare for this
Example: If your internal carbon price is ₹2,000/tCO₂e, a project that avoids 100 tonnes of emissions saves ₹2,00,000 in your calculations - making green projects more financially attractive.

Sustainability Budget: The annual budget allocated for decarbonization projects (solar installations, efficiency upgrades, EV fleet, etc.). Used by the NetZero Modeler (Enterprise tier) to optimize investment timing and calculate ROI.
🧮 How to Compute Your Internal Carbon Price
There are several methods to determine an appropriate internal carbon price for your organization. Choose the approach that best aligns with your business strategy:
1. Shadow Price Approach (Most Common)
Set a price that reflects the expected future cost of carbon regulations or offsets. This helps future-proof investment decisions.
Formula: Use projected carbon tax rates or voluntary carbon market prices
Example: If carbon offsets trade at ₹1,500/tCO₂e and you expect regulations to add 50% premium → Set internal price at ₹2,250/tCO₂e
India Reference: Current voluntary market prices range ₹1,000-₹3,000/tCO₂e
2. Cost of Abatement Approach
Calculate the average cost to reduce one tonne of CO₂ through available reduction measures (efficiency, renewables, etc.)
Formula: (Total cost of reduction project) ÷ (Total tCO₂e reduced over project lifetime)
Example: Solar installation costs ₹50,00,000, reduces 2,500 tCO₂e over 10 years → ₹2,000/tCO₂e
Application: Use the weighted average of your top 3-5 reduction opportunities
3. Social Cost of Carbon (SCC)
Use estimates of the economic damage caused by one tonne of CO₂ emissions. Reflects true societal impact.
Reference Values: US EPA estimates ~$51-$185/tCO₂ (₹4,250-₹15,400 at current rates)
Note: Academic estimate; typically higher than market prices. Best for organizations with strong sustainability commitments
4. Peer Benchmarking
Align with industry peers or leading companies in your sector
Industry Ranges: Technology: $40-100/tCO₂, Manufacturing: $20-60/tCO₂, Financial Services: $50-200/tCO₂
Source: Check CDP disclosures or sustainability reports of comparable companies
💡 Practical Tip: Start conservatively (₹1,000-₹2,000/tCO₂e) and increase over time as you build organizational capability. Many companies use tiered pricing: lower for near-term decisions, higher for long-term capital investments. Review and adjust annually based on actual abatement costs and market conditions.
Typical range: ₹1,000 - ₹5,000 per tCO₂e. Used for internal investment decisions and project evaluation in the NetZero Modeler.
