Step 2 — Assurance Risk Assessment ?
Where do risk items come from — and who rates them? Candidate rows are seeded from Step 1 (in-scope emission categories) and from the tenant's signed Management Disclosure. Every row starts unrated. The auditor then:
1. Rates Inherent Risk: how likely is an error before any controls? (High if manual data, estimates, or complex calculations)
2. Rates Control Risk: how likely is it that client internal controls miss the error? (Low if TerraCAP auto-validates; High if no review process)
3. Combined Risk = the higher of the two, and drives testing depth in Steps 4–5
Independence rule: tenant disclosures inform the auditor's judgment but never pre-populate a rating. Materiality (Step 3) is blocked until every row below is explicitly rated.
Add: Auditor can add unlisted risks (e.g. data governance, boundary disputes). Delete: Excluded categories can be removed with a rationale note.

Step 3 — Materiality Setting ?
What is materiality in GHG assurance? It defines the level of error that would cause a user of the report to reach a different decision. The auditor sets three thresholds — Overall Materiality, Performance Materiality, and Trivial Threshold — and these drive every subsequent decision about what to test and how much.
Standard: ISAE 3410 para 28; ISAE 3000 para A61
⏳ Pending Auditor Confirmation
👤 Who sets materiality — and who are these people?
Materiality is proposed by the Engagement Manager (Vikram Desai, Senior Manager at Deloitte Haskins & Sells) and approved by the Engagement Partner (Rajesh Kumar, Partner at Deloitte Haskins & Sells) — both below. Both are members of the audit firm's team, not the client. The Engagement Manager leads day-to-day fieldwork; the Partner signs the opinion and bears professional responsibility for the engagement. Client personnel (Acme's CFO, ESG Manager) are not involved in setting materiality — this must remain an independent auditor judgment.

🧭 How to choose the Overall Materiality % — this is an auditor judgment, not a system default
ISAE 3410 para 28 gives a working range of 5–10% of total reported emissions for GHG assurance (narrower than the 0.5–5% typically used for financial statement materiality, reflecting the inherent estimation uncertainty in GHG data). Within that range, move toward the lower end (5–6%) when: this is a first-year engagement, a high proportion of data is estimated (e.g. spend-based Scope 3), or Step 2 flagged multiple High combined-risk areas. Move toward the higher end (8–10%) when: the entity has multiple prior clean engagements, most data is metered/invoiced (Low risk), and controls testing supports reliance on the platform's automated checks. There is no formula that outputs this number automatically — the auditor enters it below and documents the rationale.
📐 What each threshold means and how it drives the audit
Overall Materiality (OM)
±5% = ±450 tCO₂e
If total uncorrected errors exceed OM, the auditor must qualify the opinion. The client will be asked to correct material errors before the opinion is issued. Setting it at 5% (rather than 10%) reflects the first-year risk and high proportion of estimated S3 data.
Performance Materiality (PM)
±3.5% = ±337 tCO₂e
This is the working threshold used during testing. Sample sizes are set so that the aggregate of undetected errors is unlikely to exceed PM. Tighter PM → larger samples and more tests. PM is always lower than OM to leave a buffer for errors discovered late.
Trivial Threshold
±0.5% = ±45 tCO₂e
Errors below this level are noted in working papers but not accumulated against PM. Individual items this small cannot affect the opinion. This prevents the audit from getting bogged down in very minor rounding differences.

Step 5 — Sampling Plan ?
How sample sizes are derived: Three inputs determine sample size for each area: (1) Combined Risk level from Step 2, (2) Performance Materiality from Step 3, (3) Population size. The formula: High risk → ≥25% or 100% if small population; Medium → 15–25%; Low → 10–15%. The auditor can override by entering a manual size with a justification — e.g. reducing a sample if controls testing supports reliance. Changes to risk ratings in Step 2 automatically recalculate proposed sample sizes below.

Where each column comes from (for implementation reference):
  • Population — pulled automatically from TerraCAP's live record count for that category (invoice count, log entry count, supplier count). Not editable — it reflects what's actually in the system.
  • System-Suggested Sample — calculated from the derivation rule above (risk level × population size). Shown for reference; not directly editable.
  • Auditor Sample Size — this is the field the auditor actually sets. Pre-filled with the system suggestion; the auditor can accept it as-is or change it. Reducing below the suggestion requires a justification.
  • Method — the auditor selects from a fixed list of recognised sampling methods (see column). A method is suggested based on data type and risk, but the auditor makes the final choice.
