TerraCAP has imported all facilities registered in your organisation profile. Review and confirm which are Designated Consumers (DCs) under BEE regulations. DC status is determined by sector and annual energy consumption threshold.
ℹ What is a Designated Consumer (DC)?
Under the Energy Conservation Act, any industrial plant whose annual energy consumption exceeds the notified threshold for its sector is a Designated Consumer. DCs must comply with CCTS (for notified sectors) or PAT (for transitioning sectors). Facilities below the threshold are not obligated — but can still participate in CBAM reporting if they export to the EU. TerraCAP automatically checks each facility's sector and consumption against BEE's notified thresholds and suggests DC status. You confirm or override.

Certificates & Credits — Enterprise Portfolio
Organisation-wide view of all carbon certificates across all DC facilities in the Bharathi Group. This is not limited to one facility — it aggregates CCC (from CCTS facilities) and ESCert (from PAT facilities) in one portfolio.
📖 How to read this portfolio
Why are there different sectors here? The Bharathi Group owns facilities across cement and textiles (subsidiaries) and also has a railways logistics facility under PAT. This is an organisation-level view — all group entities with DC status appear here.

CCC vs ESCert: CCC (Carbon Credit Certificates) are issued under CCTS — the new scheme. ESCert (Energy Saving Certificates) are issued under PAT — the legacy scheme. Both are real, tradeable certificates with financial value. Facilities on CCTS earn/owe CCCs; facilities on PAT earn/owe ESCerts.

What does deficit mean? If a facility's actual GHG intensity exceeds the CCTS target, it must purchase certificates from over-performers. The Chennai Textiles Unit is currently in deficit — it emits more than its target allows.
